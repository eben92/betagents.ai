# What moves a result, by sport

Generic form tables are the weakest thing you can bet on — the market has
already read them. These are the factors that carry information the price often
lags.

## Football

- **Lineups.** One missing centre-back or first-choice keeper moves a match more
  than three games of form. Confirmed team sheets appear about an hour before
  kickoff; predicted ones are worth less.
- **Suspensions.** Deterministic and public, and often mispriced in smaller
  leagues because the market is thin.
- **Fixture congestion.** A midweek European tie before a league game means
  rotation. Check the calendar, not just the squad.
- **Motivation.** Nothing to play for late in a season is a real effect.
- **Weather.** Heavy rain and strong wind suppress goals — relevant to
  `over_under` and `btts`, rarely decisive for `1x2`.

Markets: `1x2` when you have a genuine read on the result; `double_chance` when
you like a side but not enough to need the win; `over_under` and `btts` when the
information you found is about goals rather than about who wins.

## Basketball

- **Rest and travel.** Back-to-backs and long road trips are the strongest
  routine edge. Check days of rest for both sides.
- **Late injury reports.** Star availability is often confirmed close to tip-off
  and moves lines hard.
- **Pace.** Two fast teams push totals up regardless of who wins — this is where
  `over_under` earns its place.
- **Blowout risk.** A large `handicap` depends on whether starters play the
  fourth quarter, which depends on the game staying close.

Markets: `moneyline`, `over_under`, `handicap`. There is no draw.

## Cricket

- **Format first.** T20, ODI and Test behave nothing alike. Establish which you
  are looking at before anything else.
- **Pitch and conditions.** A spin-friendly surface or heavy overhead conditions
  can matter more than the teams.
- **The toss.** In some conditions it is worth a large share of the win
  probability. Note whether it has happened.
- **Availability.** International call-ups and rotation gut franchise sides
  without much notice.
- **Rain.** Reduced-overs rules change the game and can void bets entirely.

Markets: `moneyline` only. Do not nominate totals — the system cannot settle
them for cricket.

## Tennis

- **Surface.** A player's record on this surface is worth far more than their
  overall ranking.
- **Recent physical load.** A long three-setter yesterday, or a retirement in the
  last event, is a real signal.
- **Head-to-head on this surface**, not overall.
- **Withdrawals.** Late withdrawals are common; confirm the match is actually on.

Markets: `moneyline` only. There is no draw.

## Sources that tend to be worth reading

Official club and tournament sites; established sports desks; the competition's
own injury or availability report where one exists. Treat prediction sites,
tipster blogs and odds-comparison commentary as noise — they are downstream of
the price you are trying to beat.
